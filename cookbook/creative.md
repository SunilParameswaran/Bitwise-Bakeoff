# Creative Dessert Experiments

Ingredient sets evolved from real Italian dessert recipes using the size-controlled Leiden ingredient network. Independent empirical quantity samples were normalized to a 500 g known-mass starting batch; unspecified quantities are additional. gpt-5.6-terra judged the fixed candidates, then wrote methods only for selected finalists. Judge scores are subjective estimates, separate from evolutionary fitness. These recipes have not been cooked or tasted. Optional transformations and separate serving components are fallbacks; all starting amounts remain fixed.

## Dessert with Kiwi, Oat Flakes, and Red Currants

Candidate E14 · experimental · Potential 7/10 · Creativity 6/10 · Graph fitness 0.7701

Sharp fruit can balance abundant white chocolate, while oats and hazelnut flour provide structure; the kiwi-currant pairing is unusual but compatible.

### Ingredients

- 142.5 g red currants
- 34.0 g kiwi
- 10.5 g potato starch
- 201.9 g white chocolate
- 41.8 g oat flakes
- 1.2 g baking soda
- 68.1 g hazelnut flour

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the oven to 175°C. Line a small 15 cm square baking tin or similar small dish with baking parchment.
2. Melt 150 g of the white chocolate gently, then stir in the oat flakes and hazelnut flour until evenly coated.
3. Press the chocolate-oat mixture firmly into the lined tin to form a compact base.
4. Finely chop the kiwi. Mix it with the red currants, potato starch, and baking soda; spread this fruit mixture over the base.
5. Bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until the fruit is bubbling and the edges of the base look set. Cool completely in the tin so the chocolate base can firm up.
6. Melt the remaining 51.9 g white chocolate and drizzle it over the cooled dessert. Chill for 20 minutes, then cut into small squares.

### Physical-form changes

- The kiwi is finely chopped and combined with potato starch so its juices thicken during baking rather than soaking the oat-and-hazelnut base.
- White chocolate is melted to act as the only available binder for the oat and hazelnut base.

### Limitations

- The dessert will be very sweet and rich because of the high proportion of white chocolate.
- The fruit layer is soft and juicy rather than sliceable like a conventional jam filling; chill thoroughly before cutting.

## Dessert with Ground Cloves, Oat Flakes, and Red Currants

Candidate E09 · experimental · Potential 6/10 · Creativity 5/10 · Graph fitness 0.7705

Tart currants, yogurt, hazelnut, oats, and white chocolate are coherent; modest sweetness and considerable moisture make texture uncertain.

### Ingredients

- 168.4 g red currants
- 151.7 g Greek yogurt
- 24.7 g potato starch
- 42.3 g white chocolate
- 58.3 g oat flakes
- 0.8 g ground cloves
- 53.8 g hazelnut flour

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the oven to 170°C. Line a small loaf tin or 15 cm square tin with baking parchment.
2. Pulse the 58.3 g oat flakes in a food processor until they resemble a coarse flour. Mix with 53.8 g hazelnut flour, 24.7 g potato starch, and 0.8 g ground cloves.
3. Melt the 42.3 g white chocolate gently in a heatproof bowl over barely simmering water or in short microwave bursts. Let it cool until just warm.
4. Stir the 151.7 g Greek yogurt into the melted white chocolate until smooth, then stir in the dry mixture to make a thick batter.
5. Fold in all 168.4 g red currants. Spread the batter evenly in the lined tin.
6. Bake for 35 to 45 minutes, until the top is set and the centre no longer looks wet. Cool completely in the tin, then chill for at least 1 hour before slicing; this helps the potato starch and chocolate firm the dessert.

### Physical-form changes

- The oat flakes are ground into a coarse flour so they can absorb yogurt and currant juices and help form a sliceable batter.

### Limitations

- Without eggs, added sugar, or fat beyond the white chocolate, this will be a dense, softly set, tangy slice rather than a light cake. The red currants and cloves may make it notably sharp and assertive.

## Dessert with Mint, Brown Sugar, Dark Chocolate

Candidate B11 · balanced · Potential 6/10 · Creativity 5/10 · Graph fitness 0.7303

Chocolate-mint milk dessert is feasible, but the large milk load makes a soft drinkable or frozen result more credible than confectionery.

### Ingredients

- 11.9 g powdered sugar
- 288.6 g whole milk
- 107.9 g dark chocolate
- 48.1 g brown sugar
- 42.7 g butter
- 0.8 g mint

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Finely chop the mint. Chop the dark chocolate and place it in a heatproof bowl with the butter.
2. Combine the whole milk, powdered sugar, brown sugar, and chopped mint in a small heavy saucepan.
3. Bring the milk mixture just to a simmer over low heat, stirring until the sugars dissolve. Simmer gently for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until reduced to about 110 to 120 g and slightly thicker.
4. Pour the hot reduced mint milk over the dark chocolate and butter. Let stand for 1 minute, then stir from the center outward until smooth and glossy.
5. Divide the chocolate cream among small glasses or cups. Cool for 20 minutes, then refrigerate for at least 3 hours until well chilled.
6. Serve cold, stirring the dessert briefly before eating if it has separated slightly during chilling.

### Physical-form changes

- The whole milk is gently reduced to concentrate its dairy solids and lower its water content, allowing the dark chocolate and butter to form a chilled, spoonable chocolate cream without added thickeners.

### Limitations

- Without eggs, starch, gelatin, or another setting ingredient, this dessert will remain a soft chilled chocolate cream rather than a firm custard or mousse.
- The finely chopped mint remains in the dessert because straining it out would discard part of the listed ingredient.

## Dessert with Mint, Ground Cinnamon, and Unsweetened Cocoa Powder

Candidate B05 · balanced · Potential 5.5/10 · Creativity 4/10 · Graph fitness 0.7328

Cream and eggs can form a cocoa custard, but complete absence of sweetener leaves pronounced bitterness.

### Ingredients

- 357.1 g fresh liquid cream
- 1.3 g mint
- 67.1 g egg yolks
- 29.6 g unsweetened cocoa powder
- 2.1 g ground cinnamon
- 42.8 g eggs

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the 357.1 g fresh liquid cream with the 1.3 g mint in a small saucepan until steaming but not boiling. Remove from the heat and steep for 10 minutes.
2. Blend the warm cream and mint until the leaves are very finely broken down; do not strain, so all of the mint is retained.
3. Whisk the 67.1 g egg yolks and 42.8 g eggs together. Whisk in the 29.6 g unsweetened cocoa powder and 2.1 g ground cinnamon until no dry pockets remain.
4. Gradually whisk the warm mint cream into the egg mixture. Let the mixture stand for 5 minutes, then whisk again to disperse the cocoa.
5. Divide the custard among heatproof ramekins or pour into one small baking dish. Cover loosely with foil and bake at 135°C / 275°F for 25 to 35 minutes, until the edges are set and the center still trembles slightly.
6. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 3 hours before serving cold.

### Physical-form changes

- The mint is steeped and then blended into the cream, extracting its flavor while retaining the full listed quantity.

### Limitations

- This is an intentionally unsweetened cocoa custard, so it will be distinctly bitter and strongly cocoa-forward.
- Because the mint is retained rather than strained, the finished custard may contain fine green mint flecks.

## Dessert with Coffee, Strawberries, Brown Sugar

Candidate B13 · balanced · Potential 6/10 · Creativity 3.5/10 · Graph fitness 0.7298

A dense coffee-yolk dessert is feasible; cream and sugar support it, though flour is high and the small strawberry quantity contributes little.

### Ingredients

- 168.0 g fresh whipping cream
- 18.0 g strawberries
- 105.6 g egg yolks
- 93.6 g brown sugar
- 94.0 g type 00 flour
- 20.8 g coffee

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Finely dice the strawberries and set them aside for topping.
2. Warm the fresh whipping cream and coffee together in a saucepan until steaming but not boiling.
3. In a heatproof bowl, whisk the egg yolks with the brown sugar until smooth. Whisk in the type 00 flour, making a thick paste.
4. Gradually whisk the hot coffee cream into the yolk mixture, then return everything to the saucepan.
5. Cook over medium-low heat, whisking constantly, until the mixture bubbles and becomes very thick, about 3 to 5 minutes. Continue cooking for 1 minute to fully cook the flour.
6. Divide the thick coffee custard among small serving glasses or ramekins. Scatter all the strawberries over the tops, cool to room temperature, then chill for at least 3 hours before serving.

### Physical-form changes

- Heating the cream, coffee, egg yolks, and flour gelatinizes the flour and thickens the mixture into a dense, spoonable coffee custard.

### Limitations

- The high proportion of flour makes this a very dense custard rather than a light cream.
- This method assumes the coffee is prepared liquid coffee, such as espresso; dry coffee grounds cannot be properly hydrated without an unlisted liquid.
