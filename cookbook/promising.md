## Dessert with Vanilla Pod, Fresh Liquid Cream, and Icing Sugar

Candidate C12 · conservative · Potential 8/10 · Creativity 2/10 · Graph fitness 0.7496

These proportions convincingly support a vanilla-rich butter biscuit or short crust; cream adds tenderness, though sugar is relatively restrained.

### Ingredients

- 93.5 g fresh liquid cream
- 1.3 g vanilla pod
- 89.6 g sugar
- 9.2 g icing sugar
- 135.0 g butter
- 171.4 g 00 flour

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the oven to 165°C. Line a 20 cm square baking tin with baking paper.
2. Split the vanilla pod, scrape out the seeds, and finely mince the empty pod shell. Rub the seeds and minced pod into the sugar.
3. Beat the softened butter with the vanilla sugar and icing sugar for 2 to 3 minutes, until creamy.
4. Beat in the fresh liquid cream in two additions. The mixture may look slightly curdled; this is normal.
5. Fold in the 00 flour just until no dry flour remains. Spread the soft dough evenly in the lined tin.
6. Bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until pale golden at the edges and set in the centre. Cool completely in the tin, then cut into small bars.

### Physical-form changes

- The whole vanilla pod is scraped and finely minced so that both the seeds and pod shell are incorporated rather than discarded.

### Limitations

- Without eggs or chemical leavening, this is a dense, tender shortbread-style dessert rather than a light cake. The minced vanilla pod may leave noticeable fine flecks and slight fibrous texture.

## Dessert with Whole Milk, Fresh Cream, Butter

Candidate C08 · conservative · Potential 8/10 · Creativity 1/10 · Graph fitness 0.7586

The cream, egg, sugar, butter, and modest flour form a convincing rich custard-like dessert base, though its flavor profile is very plain.

### Ingredients

- 250.3 g fresh liquid cream
- 29.2 g whole milk
- 60.1 g eggs
- 22.5 g Type 00 flour
- 52.9 g butter
- 85.0 g sugar

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Whisk the sugar and Type 00 flour together in a saucepan. Whisk in the eggs and whole milk until smooth.
2. Add the fresh liquid cream and cook over medium-low heat, whisking constantly, until the mixture begins to thicken.
3. Continue cooking for 3 to 4 minutes at a gentle bubble, whisking well, until the flour is fully cooked and the cream is thick and glossy.
4. Remove from the heat. Add all the butter in small pieces and whisk until completely melted and emulsified.
5. Divide the hot cream pudding among small heatproof glasses or ramekins. Press parchment directly onto the surface if available to prevent a skin.
6. Cool for 20 minutes, then refrigerate for at least 3 hours before serving chilled.

### Physical-form changes

- The butter is melted into the cooked cream mixture, creating a richer, smoother pudding texture.

### Limitations

- Without vanilla, chocolate, fruit, salt, or other flavorings, this dessert will taste primarily of sweet dairy and butter.

## Dessert with Red Currants, Nutmeg, and Hazelnut Flour

Candidate E04 · experimental · Potential 7/10 · Creativity 5/10 · Graph fitness 0.7712

Hazelnut flour, yogurt, starch, and white chocolate support a dense dessert; redcurrant and nutmeg add a restrained, distinctive contrast.

### Ingredients

- 19.2 g red currants
- 214.7 g Greek yogurt
- 25.0 g potato starch
- 46.0 g white chocolate
- 1.6 g nutmeg
- 193.5 g hazelnut flour

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the oven to 165°C. Line a small 18 cm round cake tin or loaf tin with baking parchment.
2. Melt the 46.0 g white chocolate gently in a heatproof bowl over barely simmering water, then let it cool until just warm.
3. Whisk the 214.7 g Greek yogurt with the 25.0 g potato starch until smooth. Whisk in the melted white chocolate.
4. Stir in the 193.5 g hazelnut flour and 1.6 g nutmeg until a thick, even batter forms.
5. Fold in the 19.2 g red currants and scrape the mixture into the prepared tin. Smooth the surface.
6. Bake for 35 to 45 minutes, until the center feels set and the edges are lightly browned. Cool completely in the tin, then chill for at least 1 hour before slicing.

### Physical-form changes

- Melt the white chocolate before mixing so it disperses through the thick yogurt and hazelnut batter and helps it set when cooled.

### Limitations

- This is a dense, moist, lightly sweetened baked dessert with no added sugar or leavening; it will not have the texture of a conventional fluffy cake.
- The nutmeg quantity is assertive, while the small amount of white chocolate may not fully balance the tartness of the red currants.

## Dessert with Mint, Brown Sugar, Dark Chocolate

Candidate B16 · balanced · Potential 7/10 · Creativity 3/10 · Graph fitness 0.7270

Chocolate, milk and abundant sugars can plausibly form a very sweet fudge-like confection; mint and salt are conventional supporting accents.

### Ingredients

- 218.4 g icing sugar
- 0.5 g mint
- 63.3 g whole milk
- 159.1 g dark chocolate
- 55.8 g brown sugar
- 2.9 g fine salt

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Finely mince the 0.5 g mint and finely chop the 159.1 g dark chocolate.
2. Place the 218.4 g icing sugar, 55.8 g brown sugar, 63.3 g whole milk, and 2.9 g fine salt in a heavy saucepan. Heat gently, stirring only until the sugars are fully moistened and dissolved.
3. Bring the mixture to a steady boil over medium heat. Cook without stirring until it reaches 114–116°C (237–241°F) on a candy thermometer, about 5–8 minutes.
4. Remove from the heat and immediately stir in the chopped dark chocolate and minced mint until the chocolate is completely melted and the mixture is smooth.
5. Leave the mixture undisturbed until it cools to about 43–45°C (109–113°F), approximately 20–30 minutes. Beat vigorously with a sturdy spoon until it thickens, becomes less glossy, and starts to hold its shape.
6. Press into a small silicone-lined container, level the surface, and let set at cool room temperature for 2 hours before cutting into small pieces.

### Physical-form changes

- The mint is finely minced so its small quantity disperses evenly through the dense chocolate fudge.
- The dark chocolate is melted into cooked sugar syrup to create a firm, fudge-like confection without added fat.

### Limitations

- The fine salt quantity is high for this amount of confection and will produce a distinctly salty dark-chocolate flavor.
- Without butter or another added fat, the texture may be somewhat firm or grainy rather than creamy; accurate candy-temperature cooking and beating are important.

## Dessert with Unsweetened Cocoa Powder, Whole Milk, and Fresh Liquid Cream

Candidate C11 · conservative · Potential 7/10 · Creativity 3/10 · Graph fitness 0.7532

Rich egg-and-dairy chocolate custard or light baked pudding is plausible; modest flour supports setting, though egg proportion is high.

### Ingredients

- 80.4 g fresh liquid cream
- 103.1 g whole milk
- 3.7 g unsweetened cocoa powder
- 224.5 g eggs
- 71.5 g sugar
- 16.8 g type 00 flour

### Method (AI-generated)

1. Heat the oven to 160°C. Place a 18 cm heatproof baking dish or several small ovenproof cups on a baking tray.
2. Whisk 224.5 g eggs with 71.5 g sugar until smooth, without whipping in excessive air.
3. Sift 16.8 g type 00 flour and 3.7 g unsweetened cocoa powder over the egg mixture and whisk until no dry pockets remain.
4. Warm 103.1 g whole milk and 80.4 g fresh liquid cream together until steaming but not boiling. Gradually whisk the warm dairy into the cocoa mixture.
5. Pour the batter into the baking dish or divide it among the cups. Bake until the edges are set and the center still trembles slightly, about 30–35 minutes for one dish or 20–25 minutes for small cups.
6. Cool for 20 minutes before serving warm, or chill until cold for a firmer baked custard texture.

### Physical-form changes

- Warming the milk and cream helps disperse the small amount of cocoa powder smoothly and produces a more even custard.

### Limitations

- The cocoa quantity is low relative to the egg and dairy, so this will be a lightly cocoa-flavored, egg-forward baked custard rather than a deeply chocolate dessert.
